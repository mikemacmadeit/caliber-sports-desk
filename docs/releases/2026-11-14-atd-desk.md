# Anytime TD Desk: opportunity-based research release

Earlier live Desk selection used the highest displayed probability; newer browser-only
rules added scoring work and a price gap. Browser filters could change the chosen player.
NFL role adjustments depended on parquet files absent from
the deployment bundle; college player overlays depended on a previously exported slate.
Started-game calls were recomputed instead of reading the actual pregame decision.

The new Desk scores both leagues from Postgres history and ships separate fitted model
artifacts. It uses six recent same-season, same-team appearances, recency-weighted
carries/receptions, workload trends, offensive scoring, opponent scoring allowed by
position, and measured NFL goal-line/red-zone opportunities. A depth-chart backup is
not automatically penalized. Passing touchdowns never count toward a quarterback's ATD.
College targets, routes, red-zone and goal-line placeholders are not treated as observations.
Transferred players need current-team history. Missing inputs are visible.

The workflow borrows the opportunity/role/matchup reasoning in the anytime sections of
[Sharp Football's article](https://www.sharpfootballanalysis.com/betting/nfl-first-touchdown-scorer-bet-picks-prediction/).
It does not copy their picks, ingest their proprietary charting, or claim to have verified
their hit rate or profitability. Route participation, end-zone targets, coverage splits,
explicit quarterback-change scenarios, and market-implied team totals are not modeled
in this release. Offensive scoring rates are measured history, not market team totals.

At most one scorer is selected per game, with a 22% research floor. Known inactive,
out, doubtful, questionable, suspended or uncertain players require a pass/review.
Minimal workload, insufficient same-team history and history older than 21 days also
block selection. Unknown availability is disclosed and never treated as confirmed healthy.
Candidates also need a verified current price, at least a four-percentage-point estimated
advantage over its implied probability, and measured scoring-area work or meaningful
carry/reception volume. Recent touchdowns alone are not a scoring role. The highest
modeled chance among qualifying candidates becomes the Desk pick. The four-point margin
is a research policy, not a validated profitable threshold. Expired quotes remove a live
selection without promoting a different player in the browser. Filters and sorting do
not manufacture new Desk picks. The separate Desk section and full board are preserved.
This remains an experimental research model: no validated Play, no calibrated uncertainty
claim, and no published value recommendation from its point estimate.

## Evaluation

Regularized logistic models were fitted separately; calibration used a later historical
period. NFL training is available from 2025 (team-resolved weekly roster coverage); FBS
training uses 2023–2024, calibration 2025. The 2026 replay was not used for coefficient
fitting. Schema/identity correctness fixes were checked after inspecting it, so these
are examined research results, not a never-examined final holdout.

| 2026 probability-only replay | Opportunity model | Recent-TD baseline |
|---|---:|---:|
| NFL scorer hits | 50 / 104 (48.1%) | 54 / 104 (51.9%) |
| NFL probability Brier, lower is better | 0.1481 | 0.1683 |
| College scorer hits | 229 / 429 (53.4%) | 213 / 429 (49.7%) |
| College probability Brier | 0.1756 | 0.2034 |

The NFL candidate improves probability error but loses to the simple baseline on top
scorer hit rate. Do not market it as proven superior. A separate matched slice against
stored production forecasts has only 14 NFL games (7 vs 5 hits) and two usable college
games (1 vs 1); those samples cannot establish superiority.

These replays select by probability with a 22% floor; they do not evaluate the new
price/role/availability selection gates because historical executable prices and injury
snapshots are unavailable. They are conditional on observed skill-player participants with at least two
prior appearances. They lack historical injury availability, omitted/inactive-player
counterfactuals, and executable sportsbook prices. No ROI or prospective accuracy claim
is supported. Full metrics and sample sizes are in `2026-11-14-atd-evaluation.json`.
Raw historical records remain outside the repository; only model parameters and aggregate
evaluation results are committed. Reproduction uses `scripts/train_desk_opportunity.py`
with compact rows in `desk_opportunity.COLUMNS` order and canonical team aliases.

## Record and rollout

The scheduled publisher appends decisions, including passes, to `desk_decisions` before
kickoff. Its schema is created by the existing `init_db` startup/worker path; no table is
deleted or rewritten. Changed selections create new rows. The original price, model
artifact fingerprint, probability and rationale remain recorded. The final pregame
decision controls the scorecard. Stale quotes do not contribute to the new family's ROI.
After kickoff the board reads the recorded decision; it never retrospectively chooses
the player who scored. Earlier unrecorded games are not backfilled as new-model wins.

Both model artifacts are copied by the existing Dockerfile. API and odds publisher must
run the new release together; then the next board refresh populates the new analysis.
No new subscriptions, paid data sources or provider calls were added. Deployment was
still blocked by the previously reported manual-deployment approval check when prepared.

Validation: focused backend suite, browser selection/expiry tests, production Next build
and TypeScript. The live NFL loader produced identical replay features for 150/150
available players on the November 15 slate. Historical test success is not live accuracy.
